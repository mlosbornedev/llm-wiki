# Helium AI Infrastructure Dependency

33% of global helium supply went offline (Qatar facility hit by Iranian missiles), exposing a critical unpriced dependency in the $1.15T hyperscaler buildout.

## Key Facts
| Metric | Data |
|--------|------|
| US hyperscaler capex (2025) | $600B–$700B |
| AI infrastructure % of capex | 75% |
| Goldman Sachs projection (2025–2027) | $1.15 trillion total |
| Global helium supply offline | 33% |
| Helium container shelf life | 48 days |

## Why Helium Matters for AI
Semiconductor fabs in South Korea and Taiwan require helium for:
1. **Lithography machines** — cannot operate without it
2. **Wafer cooling** — essential for production
3. **Leak detection in vacuum chambers** — quality control

**No substitute exists for helium in any of these processes.**

## Three Impact Channels
1. **Direct supply disruption** — 33% offline, 48-day evaporation clock running
2. **LNG energy cost impact** — chip-fabricating countries ≠ data center countries; cheap energy by 2028 isn't coming on time
3. **Geopolitical restructuring** — Russia-to-China pipeline gas could advantage Chinese semiconductor manufacturing

## The Compounding Effect
Comes at the worst possible moment — into the most severe memory shortage in industry history.

## Key Insight
Every dollar of the $1.15T hyperscaler spending assumes chips arrive on schedule and fabs operate at capacity. Both are now at risk from a single point of failure in a gas facility.
