# Index Fund Forced Buying Problem

How the three massive IPOs (SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic) automatically flow into 401(k) and retirement accounts through index fund mechanics — without individual investors getting a vote.

## The Setup: Index Funds Are Automatic

An index fund buys every company in a given index. You don't pick the stocks. The index does.
- S&P 500 index fund → all 500 companies in S&P 500
- Nasdaq-100 fund → all 100 companies in Nasdaq-100

This system works well — until someone games the list.

## The Chain of Events

1. **IPO happens** — company sells 3–5% of itself to the public
2. **Fast-track index inclusion** — under [[nasdaq]], company can be added to Nasdaq-100 after just 15 trading days (effective May 1)
3. **Forced buying begins** — every fund tracking that index is **required to buy** (not encouraged, required — it's written into the fund's rules)
4. **Price spikes** — not because of any revelation about company value, but because mandatory demand meets artificially constrained supply
5. **Lock-up expires 90–180 days later** — insiders sell their 97% into the inflated price; index funds are the buyers

## The Numbers
Bloomberg Intelligence: if all three companies sell 5% of shares and get fast-tracked:
- Funds would need to buy **$24–48 billion** worth of stock
- Potentially >50% of all tradeable stock absorbed in a single week by mandatory buying

## Who Gets Hurt
- **401(k) holders** — your fund buys at the supply-constrained price without your say
- **Target-date fund holders** — picked a fund when they started a job and haven't thought about it since
- **Anyone with index funds** — the system buys for you at whatever price the market sets during historically constrained supply

The article is direct: "You don't get a vote."

## The Float Weight Illusion
Nasdaq uses total market cap ($1.75T for SpaceX) to determine index qualification. But actual index weight is capped: for companies with <20% free float, weight is capped at 5x the float percentage.

SpaceX's 3.3% float → effective weight of ~16.5% — not the full $1.75T. But still massive, because every dollar of index weight translates into billions in mandatory purchases across $30T+ in connected benchmark funds.

## The Counter-Argument
Chris Chandler (commenter) notes: passive funds represent ~33% of market activity (2023 HBS paper cited). Professional money managers measured against indexes fall behind if they don't own what the index owns — so "voluntary" index-hugging behavior amplifies the forced buying further.
