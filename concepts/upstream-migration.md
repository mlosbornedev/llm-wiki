# Upstream Migration

The pattern: when AI collapses a gap, value migrates to the next gap upstream. The new gap is always closer to judgment, taste, relationships, and system-level thinking — further from production, execution, and information retrieval.

## The CNC Lathe Parallel

1980s: CNC machining arrives. A shop owner buys a CNC lathe, hires a less-skilled operator, produces precision parts at a fraction of the hand-milling time. Smart shops hid the machines and charged the old rate. Margin was enormous. For a while.

Then everyone got CNC machines. Prices for many machined components dropped dramatically. The bespoke premium evaporated because precision was no longer a rare skill. It was a machine setting.

**That's the exact arc playing out in every knowledge-work industry right now.** The agencies and consultancies using AI to produce at a fraction of the old cost while charging the old rate are living inside that window. It's real and profitable. And it's closing.

## The Pattern in Practice

**Legal research:**
- Gap: finding the right precedent was historically expensive
- AI closes: legal research gets commoditized toward zero
- New gap: judgment and client trust. The research is free. The counsel is not.

**Financial analysis:**
- Gap: compiling data and running regressions
- AI closes: data gathering and basic analysis compress toward zero
- New gap: knowing which regression matters, interpreting in context, making defensible recommendations

**Code generation:**
- Gap: producing working functions
- AI closes: anyone can generate code
- New gap: system design and integration. Anyone can generate functions; not everyone can architect systems that work reliably at scale.

**Content production:**
- Gap: producing content was expensive
- AI closes: production cost collapses
- New gap: distribution and taste. Anyone can produce; not everyone can reach an audience or curate quality.

## The Migration Path Is Predictable

The new gap is always **upstream of the old one:**

```
Old gap: Can you produce it?
New gap: Do you know what to produce, for whom, and why?
```

This means the migration path is visible in advance. Once you see the pattern, you can trace where value is heading in your own industry before it gets there.

## The Practical Implication

**The junior analyst problem:**

A junior financial analyst's job is ~70% data gathering/formatting, 20% analysis, 10% judgment. AI collapses the 70% toward zero.

Naive conclusion: you need fewer analysts.

Better conclusion: the analyst role migrates upstream. The same person, freed from gathering and formatting, spends 60% on analysis and 40% on judgment. The gap shifts from "who can compile the data?" to "who can interpret data in context and make a defensible recommendation?"

That second gap is harder to close — it requires domain knowledge, institutional context, and integrative reasoning that current models do adequately but not reliably.

## The Judgment Paradox (from reader comment)

Cullen Steele raised a critical challenge the article doesn't fully address:

The grunt work IS the training ground for judgment. The junior analyst building the 70% was developing the pattern recognition, domain intuition, and feel for when something is off — that makes the 30% possible. You can't skip the reps and expect the skill to develop.

**Aristotle called this phronesis (practical wisdom)** — develops only through making judgments in particular situations over time, encountering consequences, integrating what you learned. There is no shortcut.

The paradox: the market demands wisdom (the upstream gap). AI removes the conditions under which wisdom forms (the productive struggle of the grunt work). This may be a civilizational problem, not just a skills gap.
