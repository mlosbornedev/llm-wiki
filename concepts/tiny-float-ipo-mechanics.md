# Tiny Float IPO Mechanics

The playbook all three companies (SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic) are using to raise enormous sums while keeping the vast majority of their equity off the public market.

## The Standard IPO Float
Typically, a company going public puts 15–25% of its shares on the open market. That "float" is what you and I can actually trade. The rest stays with founders, employees, and early investors.

## The Tiny Float Playbook
Instead, SpaceX and peers plan to float only ~3–5% of the company.

### Why It Works Mathematically
SpaceX wants to raise $50–75B at a $1.75T valuation:
- At a normal 25% float → market would need to cough up $450B
- At 3.3% float → market needs to absorb only $50–75B

The math works on paper. You don't need a $450B market appetite. You just need enough to cover the raise.

### The Consequence: Price Reflects Scarcity, Not Value
When constrained supply meets overwhelming demand → prices go haywire. It's the Ticketmaster problem applied to stock markets.

Analogy from the article:
> If a restaurant has 200 seats and 250 people want a reservation, the experience is roughly normal. If a restaurant has 10 seats and 10,000 people want in, you're not paying for dinner anymore. You're paying for access.

PitchBook analysis on SpaceX specifically: at 3.3% float, expect 20–30% single-day price swings — roughly double Tesla's volatility.

## The Index Fund Amplification
This is where it gets systemic. With [[nasdaq]] in place:
1. Company floats 3–5% in June
2. Three weeks later, added to Nasdaq-100 weighted at ~5x float (capped)
3. Every index fund tracking Nasdaq-100 **required to buy**
4. Trillions of dollars of mandatory buying competing for the same 3–5% pool

Bloomberg Intelligence: if all three sell 5% and get fast-tracked → $24–48B in mandatory fund buying, potentially absorbing >50% of all available tradeable stock within a single week.

## The Lock-Up Expiration Problem
The 97% that isn't available gets unlocked 90–180 days after IPO:
- Founders, early investors, employees can now sell
- They're selling into the artificially inflated price
- The buyers: index funds, 401(k) holders, people who had no choice

This is described in the article as a wealth transfer from (retail/index fund holders) to (insiders who bought at pre-IPO valuations).
