# The Judgment Paradox

A tension raised by reader Cullen Steele that the original article doesn't resolve: AI removes the difficulty that is the mechanism for developing judgment.

## The Paradox

The market demands wisdom (the [[upstream-migration|upstream gap]]). AI removes the conditions under which wisdom forms (the productive struggle of foundational work).

Aristotle called it **phronesis** — practical wisdom. It develops only through:
1. Making judgments in particular situations
2. Encountering consequences
3. Integrating what you learned over time

There is no shortcut. The difficulty is not an obstacle to developing judgment — it is the mechanism.

## The Specific Case

The junior analyst spending 70% of their time compiling data:
- Wasn't wasting time
- Was building pattern recognition
- Was developing domain intuition
- Was learning to feel when something is off in the numbers

That "grunt work" was the training ground that made the remaining 30% (judgment calls) possible.

If AI eliminates the 70%, what happens to the pipeline that develops the 30%?

## The Implication

A working generation from now, there may be people empowered to produce superhuman output, but lacking the foundational capacity to evaluate whether that output is any good.

**This isn't a skills gap. It may be a civilizational problem.**

## The Article's Position (Unresolved)

The article acknowledges the paradox but doesn't resolve it — it points toward [[upstream-migration]] as the migration path without addressing how people develop the upstream skills if they've never done the downstream reps.

The question the article raises but can't answer: **who's building the pipeline that gets people to judgment if we've automated the first ten years of reps?**
