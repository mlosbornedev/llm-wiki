# Discipline Gaps

One of the [[arbitrage-gaps-taxonomy|five gap types]]. The inefficiency isn't in the market or the information. It's in the human executing on it.

## The Mechanism

AI doesn't close discipline gaps by replacing the human. It closes them by **enforcing the consistency the human can't maintain alone.**

Same strategy, bots vs. humans: roughly **double the profit**. Not because the bots had better judgment. Because execution never degraded over 6,000 consecutive decisions.

Human execution degrades with:
- Fatigue (3 AM trading)
- Emotion (oversized positions on "confident" bets)
- Boredom (missed trades during lunch)
- Backlog (other things to read)

AI has none of these constraints.

## Examples Across Industries

**Sales:** Team knows the playbook but doesn't follow it consistently. AI enforces the playbook on every call, every email, every follow-up.

**Content:** Pipeline produces erratic quality depending on who's working that day. AI enforces consistency.

**Operations:** Team drifts from protocol under pressure. AI maintains protocol regardless of circumstances.

**Investing:** 6,615 trades, each precisely calibrated. Humans knew what to do. They just couldn't do it consistently.

## The Discipline Gap vs. Knowledge Gap

The [[discipline-gaps]] (labor arbitrage → intelligence arbitrage) is about **what you know**. The discipline gap is about **what you do with what you know**, consistently, at scale.

These gaps compress differently:
- Knowledge gap: closes when information becomes universally accessible (every model release)
- Discipline gap: closes when execution becomes automated (every workflow automation)
