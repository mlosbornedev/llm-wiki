# Three IPO Scenarios

What happens next with SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic IPOs. The article offers no prediction — just three mutually exclusive outcomes and the conditions that would drive each.

## Scenario 1: Floats Stay Tiny, Insiders Wait Years to Sell
- The 3% float persists. Insiders hold their 97% and wait.
- This **preserves headline valuations** — SpaceX at $1.75T, OpenAI at $1T+
- But: a stock price set by 3% of the company trading hands is **not the same** as a price set by an open market
- If you hold equity at one of these companies, your statement number is life-changing — but you can't spend it until the market actually agrees to pay that price at scale
- This could take **years**
- Insiders stay locked in; public shareholders hold a tiny, artificially-priced float

## Scenario 2: Valuations Come Down at S-1 Filing
- An S-1 filing is a moment of truth — first time these companies show real financials to the public
- For [[openai]]: $14B loss in 2026, $57B burn by 2027, profitability not until 2030
- For [[anthropic]]: potential accounting problem with cloud credits booked as revenue
- If the numbers don't match the narrative, the market reprices them
- The article's author lean: "My instinct says the valuations come down" — the pricing structure is artificially inflated by design and structures like that tend to correct

## Scenario 3: Forced Buying Creates Self-Reinforcing Bubble
- Index inclusion drives mandatory buying
- Mandatory buying drives price up
- Higher price validates the valuation
- Validated valuation drives more index weight → more mandatory buying
- If it works: valuations maintained through **structural demand rather than fundamental analysis**
- The article's framing: "Self-reinforcing loops in financial markets have a name. They're called **bubbles**."
- And bubbles have a predictable second act

## The Author's Position
> "I'll be honest — I don't know which of these wins, and I'd be cautious about anyone who says they do."

The forced-buying mechanism is "genuinely new. We haven't seen index rules weaponized at this scale before, and the people designing this structure are not amateurs."
