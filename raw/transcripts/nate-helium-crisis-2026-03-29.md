## Executive Summary
The largest debt-funded infrastructure buildout in history has an unpriced dependency on a noble gas. A missile strike on Qatar's helium facility (supplying one-third of global helium) exposed a critical vulnerability in AI infrastructure planning.

## Critical Facts
- US hyperscaler capex (2025): $600B–$700B
- AI infrastructure % of capex: 75%
- Goldman Sachs projection (2025–2027): $1.15 trillion total
- Global helium supply affected: 33%
- Helium container shelf life: 48 days

## Why Helium Matters
Semiconductor fabs in South Korea and Taiwan require helium for:
1. Lithography machines — cannot operate without it
2. Wafer cooling — essential for production
3. Leak detection in vacuum chambers — quality control requirement

**There is no substitute for helium in any of these processes.**

## What Happened
- Qatar's industrial complex (1/3 of global supply) was hit by Iranian missiles three weeks ago
- Facility is offline with parts destroyed
- Shipping strait is closed
- Specialized liquid helium containers vaporize contents within 48 days

## The Three Impact Channels
1. **Direct Helium Supply Disruption** — 33% of world supply offline, no viable substitute
2. **LNG Energy Cost Impact** — Countries that fabricate chips are not the countries that run data centers; LNG expansion supposed to make energy cheap by 2028 isn't coming on time
3. **Geopolitical Restructuring** — Russia-to-China pipeline gas could structurally advantage Chinese semiconductor manufacturing

## The Memory Compounding Effect
The helium disruption arrives at the worst possible moment — into a semiconductor supply chain already in the grip of the most severe memory shortage in industry history.
