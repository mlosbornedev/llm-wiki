## Summary

SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic (collectively valued at $3T+) are planning IPOs within 12 months, seeking to raise $170–195B against a market that handled $47B in IPOs all of last year. All three use a tiny-float strategy (~3% of company on open market), making prices reflect scarcity not value. Nasdaq rewrote index rules (effective May 1) to allow inclusion after just 15 trading days. Every index fund (401k, target-date, etc.) will be forced to buy these stocks automatically at whatever price the market sets. Lock-up expirations 90-180 days later allow insiders to sell into that artificially high price while index funds are holding.

Financial details:
- SpaceX: ~$1.75T valuation, planning $50-75B raise, 3.3% float expected, acquired xAI (burning $1B/month)
- OpenAI: $1T+ valuation, planning $60B+ raise, losing $14B in 2026, burn to reach $57B by 2027, profitable 2030. Stargate project failed to get debt financing; now renting AWS/Google Cloud instead
- Anthropic: $60B+ raise planned, cloud credits from Amazon/Google booked as revenue ($1.9B 2026, $6.4B 2027 projected) — potential accounting problem if regulators force change
- Bloomberg Intelligence: if all three sell 5% float and get fast-tracked, $24-48B in mandatory buying needed from funds

Three scenarios: floats stay tiny (valuations aspirational), valuations come down (S-1 truth moment), forced buying creates self-reinforcing bubble loop.
