## Core Thesis
> "OpenClaw is the most consequential provocation in AI since ChatGPT."

Every major company responding to OpenClaw made a fundamentally different bet on the same set of tradeoffs. Nobody is responding the same way, and that divergence is the real story.

## The Three-Axis Evaluation Framework
| Axis | Question | Range |
|------|----------|-------|
| Infrastructure | Where does the agent run? | Cloud ↔ Local |
| Model Selection | Who picks the model? | Platform-controlled ↔ User-controlled |
| User Assumption | What does the interface assume about you? | Expert ↔ Novice |

## The Five Strategic Plays

### 1. OpenClaw's Sovereignty Bet
Self-hosted, user-controlled approach — comparably significant to Linux's emergence (per Nvidia).

### 2. Perplexity's Delegation Play
First moved: shipped a $200/month cloud agent. Two weeks later: shipped a second product (local agent on dedicated Mac). Cloud alone "wasn't close enough."

### 3. Meta's Distribution Move
$2 billion investment in Manus — leverage existing user base.

### 4. Anthropic's Safety Positioning
Product: Dispatch — emphasis on safety and controlled execution.

### 5. Lovable's Expansion
Most copied AI product of 2025. $400M ARR, $6.6B valuation. Expanding beyond app building → general-purpose agent tasks.

## How to Evaluate Any New Agent
1. Where does it run? (Cloud vs. local)
2. Who controls the model? (Platform vs. user)
3. What does it assume about me? (Expert vs. novice)

## Key Insight
The AI agent landscape is fragmenting rather than consolidating. The floor (reliability/security) matters more than the ceiling (features).
