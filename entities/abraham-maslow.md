# Abraham Maslow

American psychologist who created the hierarchy of needs. Founder of humanistic psychology. His work on self-actualization provides one of the developmental foundations for Human 3.0.

## Key Contributions

### The Hierarchy of Needs
Often misrepresented as a rigid ladder. Maslow's actual research showed through studies of self-actualizing individuals that higher needs emerge naturally once lower needs are reasonably satisfied — not perfectly met, just "good enough." This is why Human 3.0 uses "minimum effective dose" rather than demanding full baseline security before growth.

### Self-Actualization
Studied exemplary individuals (Lincoln, Einstein, Eleanor Roosevelt, etc.) to understand what separates truly developed humans from the rest. Found they shared: comfort with ambiguity, spontaneous and private, autonomous, continued freshness of appreciation, Gemeinschaftsgefühl (social interest), deep relationships but comfortable with solitude, non-hostile sense of humor.

### Being Needs vs. Deficiency Needs
Distinguished between D-needs (deficiency needs: food, safety, love, esteem — triggered by absence) and B-needs (being needs: truth, goodness, beauty — triggered by presence). Human 3.0's levels map roughly to movement from D-needs toward B-needs.

## Role in Human 3.0

- Hierarchy validates the quadrant approach (needs must be met in parallel, not series)
- "Good enough" baseline principle prevents perfectionism as obstacle
- Self-actualization research provides the template for Level 3.0+ development
- The progression from deficiency to being maps to Vocation stages (Job → Career → Calling)
