# Nasdaq

Stock exchange that rewritten its index rules to enable fast-track inclusion of massive IPOs — directly causing retirement accounts to be forced into these stocks.

## The Rule Change (Effective May 1)
Under the **old rules**: a company had to trade publicly for months, sometimes a full year, before being added to a major index. The logic: let the market figure out what the company is actually worth before forcing every index fund to buy it.

Under the **new rules**: a company as large as SpaceX can be added to the Nasdaq-100 after just **15 trading days** (3 calendar weeks).

## Why Nasdaq Did This
SpaceX reportedly made fast-track index inclusion a **condition for choosing Nasdaq over the New York Stock Exchange**. The company understood exactly what it was asking for — and got it.

## How the Weight Works
- Nasdaq uses **total market cap** ($1.75T for SpaceX) to determine index qualification
- But for companies with less than 20% free float, weight is **capped at 5x the float percentage**
- SpaceX's 3.3% float → effective weight of ~16.5% (not full $1.75T)
- Still massive: $30T+ in fund assets are connected to these benchmarks

## S&P and FTSE Russell Following
S&P is considering similar fast-track rules for S&P 500. FTSE Russell doing the same for its indexes.

See [[index-fund-forced-buying]] — this rule change is what makes the IPO mechanically flow into every 401(k) in the country within three weeks of going public.
